
1. OVERVIEW / INTRODUCTION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Jassids (Cotton Leaf Hoppers), scientifically known as Amrasca biguttula biguttula 
(Ishida), are one of the most destructive sucking pests of cotton in South Asia. 
They are small, wedge-shaped insects belonging to the family Cicadellidae 
(Order: Hemiptera).

Jassids are a PERENNIAL, SEASON-LONG THREAT in cotton fields of both Pakistan 
and ''. In Pakistan alone, jassid infestations cause an estimated 15–40% 
reduction in seed cotton yield every season when left unmanaged. In '' 
(particularly in the non-Bt cotton zones of Punjab and Haryana), the damage 
can exceed 30% during peak infestation.

Scientific Classification:
  Kingdom  : Animalia
  Phylum   : Arthropoda
  Class    : Insecta
  Order    : Hemiptera
  Family   : Cicadellidae
  Genus    : Amrasca
  Species  : A. biguttula biguttula

Common Names: Jassid (Pakistan/''), Cotton Leaf Hopper, Lal Teela (Punjabi), 
              Tela (Sindhi), Leafhopper (English)

2. IDENTIFICATION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Adult Jassid:
  - Size: 2.5–3.5 mm long
  - Color: Pale yellowish-green with two black spots on the forewing
  - Shape: Wedge-shaped, wider at head, tapering toward rear
  - Moves: Runs sideways quickly when disturbed (distinctive crab-like movement)
  - Wings: Hold wings tent-like over body at rest

Nymphs (immature):
  - Wingless, pale green
  - 5 nymphal instars (stages) before becoming adult
  - More damaging per individual than adults during feeding

Eggs:
  - Inserted into leaf veins (endophytic) — invisible to naked eye
  - Hatch in 7–10 days depending on temperature
  - Pakistan: Peak egg laying April–June and again August–September

Life Cycle Duration:
  - Egg to adult: 16–24 days (faster in hot weather 35–42°C)
  - Adults live: 20–30 days
  - Generations per season: 8–12 overlapping generations
  - Pakistan: April–October is peak activity season

3. DAMAGE SYMPTOMS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

How Jassids Cause Damage:
  - Both adults and nymphs feed on undersides of cotton leaves
  - Insert stylet (needle-like mouthpart) into phloem tissue
  - Inject toxic saliva that disrupts phloem transport
  - Extract phloem sap (nutrients and sugars)

Progressive Damage Stages:
  Stage 1 (Early): Yellowing at leaf margins; small triangular yellow patches at tips
  Stage 2 (Moderate): Yellow patches enlarge; leaves curl downward and inward
  Stage 3 (Severe - "Hopper Burn"): 
    - Leaf margins turn brown/reddish-brown (scorched appearance)
    - Leaf curl severe — looks like "dried tobacco leaf"
    - Leaves become crisp, brittle, and eventually fall off
  Stage 4 (Critical): Complete defoliation of lower canopy, 
    stunted plant growth, boll shedding, severe yield loss

Economic Threshold (Action Level):
  - Pakistan: 1–2 nymphs per leaf (counted on 3 leaves per plant, 5 plants/acre)
  - '': Economic Threshold Level (ETL) = 2 jassids per leaf
  - Above threshold: Immediate control measures required

4. FACTORS FAVORING JASSID BUILDUP
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Environmental Factors:
  - Hot, dry weather (35–45°C) with low humidity favors rapid multiplication
  - Pakistan's May–July period provides ideal conditions
  - Overcast, humid weather slows population growth
  - Excessive nitrogen fertilization (lush green growth attracts more jassids)
  - Dense plant populations (overcrowded fields)

Cultural Factors:
  - Late planting misses the window for resistant varieties
  - Susceptible varieties (smooth leaf, high nitrogen uptake)
  - Nearby alternate host plants (okra, tomato, brinjal, weeds)
  - Killing of natural enemies through excessive insecticide use
  - Irrigated fields with lush growth more attractive

5. PREVENTION STRATEGIES — DETAILED (THE CORE FOCUS)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

5.1 RESISTANT VARIETY SELECTION (First Line of Defense)
---------------------------------------------------------
Cotton varieties with hairy leaves (pubescent/okra leaf) naturally resist jassids.
The tiny hairs (trichomes) on leaf surfaces impede nymph movement and feeding.

PAKISTAN — Recommended Jassid-Tolerant Varieties:
  - CRSM-38 (Central Cotton Research Station Multan)
  - FH-142, FH-152 (Faisalabad Hybrid — moderate resistance)
  - Sitara-008 (high yield, moderate resistance)
  - MNH-886, MNH-1016 (Multan Nuclear Holdings)
  - IUB-222 (Islamia University Bahawalpur)
  - NIAB-512 (hairy leaf type — good jassid resistance)
  Note: Okra-leaf and sub-okra leaf types have highest resistance

'' — Recommended Jassid-Tolerant Varieties:
  - LH-1556, LH-2108 (Punjab Agricultural University, Ludhiana)
  - HD-107 (Haryana)
  - Suraj (Gujarat — high hair density)
  - Khandwa-2 (hairy leaf type, CICR recommended)
  - JKCH-1947 (Bt hybrid with moderate jassid tolerance)
  - Vikram (Rajasthan, hairy leaf character)

Variety Selection Criteria:
  ✓ High leaf hair density (pubescence)
  ✓ Okra or sub-okra leaf shape (reduces microclimate for pest)
  ✓ Thick leaf cuticle
  ✓ Fast canopy closure (reduces exposed leaf area time)

5.2 TIMELY SOWING
------------------
Pakistan:
  - Optimal sowing: April 15 – May 15 in Punjab
  - Sindh: March 15 – April 30
  - Early sowing ensures plant is at boll-set stage before peak jassid July–August
  - Very late sowing (June+) means young leaves exposed during peak pest season

'':
  - Punjab/Haryana: Sow by May 15–June 15
  - Vidarbha: June 1–July 15 (post-monsoon onset)
  - Avoid late sowing — jassid populations peak August–September

5.3 PROPER PLANT SPACING & DENSITY
-------------------------------------
  - Avoid overcrowding: Recommended spacing 75cm × 30cm (row × plant)
  - Dense canopy creates humid microclimate that slows jassid spread, BUT
  - Overcrowded fields: poor air circulation = pesticide penetration difficulty
  - Recommended plant population: 16,000–20,000 plants/acre (Pakistan)
  - Thin excess plants within 3 weeks of emergence

5.4 WEED MANAGEMENT AS PREVENTION
------------------------------------
  - Remove alternate host weeds: Okra-family weeds, Malvastrum (false mallow), 
    Sida sp. (Bakra buti), and Hibiscus weeds harbor jassid populations
  - Clear field borders of weeds before planting season
  - Use narrow inter-row cultivation to destroy weed hosts
  - Pakistan: Chaulai (Amaranthus) and Lana weeds also host jassids — remove

5.5 FERTILIZER MANAGEMENT
----------------------------
  - Avoid EXCESSIVE nitrogen: Lush, soft, nitrogen-rich foliage is preferred 
    by jassids — it's more tender and easier to pierce
  - Balanced NPK: For cotton in Pakistan, recommended ratio 2:1:1 (N:P:K)
  - Split nitrogen applications: Do not apply large single doses
  - Apply potash (K2O) at 40–50 kg/acre — potassium increases cell wall 
    strength, making leaves less favorable for jassid feeding
  - Avoid urea broadcasting in humid/rainy weather — leaches and causes lush 
    vulnerable growth flush

5.6 CONSERVATION OF NATURAL ENEMIES
--------------------------------------
Jassid natural enemies in Pakistan & '' cotton:
  A. Predators:
     - Chrysoperla carnea (Green lacewing) — larvae eat jassid eggs and nymphs
     - Coccinella septempunctata (Ladybird beetle) 
     - Orius sp. (Minute pirate bug)
     - Spiders (wolf spider, crab spider) — general predators on leaves
  
  B. Parasitoids:
     - Gonatocerus sp. (egg parasitoid)
     - Anagrus atomus (egg parasitoid)
  
  C. Entomopathogenic fungi:
     - Beauveria bassiana (naturally infects jassids during humid periods)
     - Metarhizium anisopliae

  HOW TO CONSERVE NATURAL ENEMIES:
     ► Avoid broad-spectrum insecticides (organophosphates, pyrethroids) 
       in the first 45 days of crop — most natural enemies establish early
     ► Use selective/targeted insecticides only (imidacloprid seed treatment)
     ► Maintain flowering border crops (coriander, fennel) to support 
       parasitoid wasps
     ► Reduce spray frequency — spray only when ETL is exceeded

5.7 PHYSICAL & CULTURAL PREVENTION MEASURES
----------------------------------------------
  A. Sticky Yellow Traps:
     - Install yellow sticky traps @ 8–10 traps/acre to monitor adult population
     - Place at plant canopy level
     - Replace every 2 weeks
     - Acts as early warning system — spray only when trap counts rise sharply

  B. Light Traps:
     - UV light traps attract adult jassids at night
     - Set up 1 trap per 4–5 acres
     - Provides population trend data for spray timing decisions

  C. Reflective Mulch:
     - Silver/aluminum reflective plastic mulch along rows confuses jassid 
       orientation and reduces colonization
     - Used primarily in small-scale/kitchen garden cotton settings
     - Less practical for large commercial fields in Pakistan/''

5.8 SEED TREATMENT AS PREVENTIVE MEASURE
------------------------------------------
  - Imidacloprid 70% WS seed treatment @ 5–7 g/kg seed
    Provides systemic protection for first 35–45 days
    Highly effective against early-season jassid colonization
  - Thiamethoxam 30% FS @ 10 ml/kg seed — alternative to imidacloprid
  - IMPORTANT: Seed treatment protection does NOT last full season — 
    field monitoring still required after 45 days

  Pakistan: Seed treatment is mandated by CCRI for certified cotton seed lots
  '': Included in package of practices by state agriculture departments

5.9 BOTANICAL / BIO-PESTICIDE PREVENTION
------------------------------------------
For organic or low-input farming systems:
  A. Neem-based products:
     - Neem oil (Azadirachtin 1500 ppm) @ 5 ml/liter — apply fortnightly
     - Neem Seed Kernel Extract (NSKE) 5% — disrupt jassid feeding and molting
     - Apply in evening — neem degrades in UV light

  B. Beauveria bassiana (entomopathogenic fungus):
     - Commercial products: BioKill, Mycotal (''), Marvelous BB (Pakistan)
     - Apply at 5×10^9 spores/ml spray @ 2–3 ml/liter
     - Best applied during humid/cloudy conditions (>70% relative humidity)
     - Infects jassid nymphs and adults within 5–7 days

  C. Verticillium lecanii:
     - Applied as foliar spray — targets soft-bodied insects including jassids
     - Dose: 2×10^8 spores/ml

5.10 INTERCROPPING TO REDUCE JASSID PRESSURE
----------------------------------------------
  - Intercrop cotton with: Cowpea (lobia), Moong, or Clusterbean (guar)
    These attract natural enemies and provide diversified habitat
  - Trap crop: Plant small plots of susceptible okra around cotton field borders
    Jassids concentrate on okra border → destroy/spray okra plots selectively
  - Pakistan Sindh farmers use jowar (sorghum) windbreaks that also reduce drift 
    and provide habitat for spiders and other predators

6. MONITORING PROTOCOL
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Weekly Scouting Method:
  1. Select 20 plants randomly across the field (diagonal transect pattern)
  2. On each plant: examine 3 leaves (upper, middle, lower canopy)
  3. Count NYMPHS only (nymphs are more indicative of infestation level)
  4. Record: nymphs per leaf per plant
  5. Calculate average nymphs/leaf
  6. Action threshold: ≥2 nymphs/leaf = spray required

Best Scouting Time: Morning (7–10 AM) when jassids are active
Avoid scouting: During hot afternoon (insects less visible)
