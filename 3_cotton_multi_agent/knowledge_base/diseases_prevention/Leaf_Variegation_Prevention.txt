
1. OVERVIEW / INTRODUCTION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Leaf Variegation in cotton refers to an irregular pattern of mixed green and 
yellow/white areas on cotton leaves, creating a mottled, mosaic, or two-toned 
appearance. The condition departs from the normal uniform green color of healthy 
leaves, and can arise from multiple causes ranging from viral infections to 
nutrient disorders to genetic characteristics.

Unlike reddening (which is stress-related anthocyanin pigmentation), variegation 
specifically describes irregular chlorophyll distribution — resulting in patches, 
streaks, or sectors of green and non-green tissue on the same leaf.

Types of Variegation in Cotton:
  1. Viral Mosaic (Cotton Mosaic Virus, CLCuV mosaic symptoms)
  2. Nutrient Deficiency Mosaics (Iron, Manganese, Zinc)
  3. Genetic/Chimeric Variegation (not a disease — heritable)
  4. Herbicide-Induced Chlorosis (discussed in herbicide document)
  5. Phytoplasma-Associated Variegation

The most economically important cause in Pakistan and '' is:
  → Cotton Leaf Curl Virus (CLCuV) mosaic component, and
  → Iron/Zinc/Manganese micronutrient deficiency mosaics

Pakistan-specific concern: CLCuV has caused EPIDEMIC losses in Punjab since 1988.
''-specific concern: Zinc deficiency variegation is extremely widespread in 
Indo-Gangetic cotton areas.

2. TYPES & IDENTIFICATION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

2.1 Cotton Leaf Curl Virus (CLCuV) — Mosaic/Variegation Component
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Pathogen: Cotton Leaf Curl Virus (genus Begomovirus, family Geminiviridae)
Vector: Bemisia tabaci (whitefly — Biotype B/Asia-II)
Transmission: Persistent, circulative — acquired in 4–8 hours of feeding

Symptoms causing variegation:
  - Yellow-green mosaic/mottling on young leaves
  - Irregular light and dark green patches
  - Interveinal yellowing similar to nutrient deficiency (but uniform across plant)
  - Accompanied by: Upward leaf curl, vein thickening, leaf enations (small 
    leaf-like outgrowths on underside), stunted growth
  
Key Diagnostic Test for CLCuV:
  - Bend leaf upward — if veins appear swollen/thickened = CLCuV likely
  - Check leaf underside for enations (warty leaf protrusions) 
  - PCR test available at AARI, NIBGE (National Institute for Biotechnology 
    and Genetic Engineering), Faisalabad — definitive diagnosis

2.2 Zinc Deficiency Variegation
---------------------------------
  - Very common in Pakistan (alkaline soils, pH>7.8) and '' (flooded paddy 
    fields preceding cotton in rotation)
  - Symptoms:
    * New (young) leaves show interveinal chlorosis — small green veins on 
      pale yellow/cream background
    * Older leaves may appear bronze-green
    * Distinctively: Leaves become small, crinkled, distorted
    * Terminal bud growth severely stunted
    * In Pakistan Punjab: Called "Little Leaf" syndrome when zinc very deficient
  - Cause: Zinc forms insoluble zinc hydroxide/carbonate in alkaline soils
  - pH>7.5 makes zinc nearly unavailable regardless of total soil zinc content

2.3 Iron Deficiency Chlorosis (Variegation-Like)
-------------------------------------------------
  - Young (new) leaves develop intense interveinal chlorosis
  - Veins remain bright green against a bright yellow background (striking contrast)
  - Older leaves stay green (iron is immobile — deficiency shows in new growth first)
  - '': Calcareous soils of Rajasthan; Pakistan: Irrigated soils with poor drainage
  - Cause: Waterlogging, high bicarbonate in irrigation water (Pakistan canal water),
    or excessive phosphorus application (blocks iron uptake)

2.4 Manganese Deficiency
--------------------------
  - Young leaves: Gray-green interveinal chlorosis (less yellow than zinc/iron)
  - Veins remain green; tissue between veins becomes pale
  - Whole leaf may appear speckled/variegated
  - Common in leached, acidic sandy soils (rare in Pakistan's alkaline areas)
  - More common in '''s red laterite soils of Andhra/Telangana cotton zones

2.5 Genetic / Chimeric Variegation
-------------------------------------
  - Some cotton plants naturally show mosaic-like yellow-green sectors on leaves
  - This is a GENETIC CHIMERA — not caused by disease or deficiency
  - Important to identify: Plants do not show other disease symptoms, yield is normal
  - Identifiable: Consistent pattern on same plant season after season
  - Prevention: Choose certified, genetically stable varieties from reputable sources

2.6 Cotton Mosaic Virus (CMV) — Rare in Pakistan/'' Cotton
--------------------------------------------------------------
  - Caused by Cucumber Mosaic Cucumovirus (CMV)
  - Symptoms: Yellow-green mosaic, distortion, leaf size reduction
  - Vector: Multiple aphid species (non-persistent transmission)
  - Less common in commercial cotton than CLCuV in this region

3.1 PREVENTION OF CLCuV-RELATED VARIEGATION
--------------------------------------------

A. RESISTANT VARIETY USE (Most Critical)
   CLCuV-Resistant varieties for Pakistan:
     - IUB-222 (high resistance, good yield)
     - FH-152, FH-142 (moderate-high resistance)
     - NIAB-512 (NIAB Faisalabad)
     - CIM-598, CIM-602 (Central Cotton Research Institute Multan)
     - BS-2015, BS-52 (recently released with improved resistance)
   
   CLCuV-Tolerant varieties for '':
     - Suraj, Khandwa-2 (for Central '' zones)
     - LH-2108 (Punjab)
     - Dharwad variety selections from UAS Dharwad
   
   NOTE: New CLCuV strains (Burewala strain, New Delhi strain) have overcome 
   resistance in some older varieties. Always use latest recommended varieties.

B. WHITEFLY VECTOR MANAGEMENT (Key Prevention Lever)
   Since CLCuV is transmitted exclusively by whitefly, reducing whitefly 
   population directly reduces virus spread:

   i. Yellow Sticky Traps:
      - Install at 8–10 traps/acre, at canopy height
      - Change fortnightly; monitor to track population trends
      - Acts as both monitor AND control tool
   
   ii. Reflective Silver Mulch:
      - Confuses whitefly orientation (cannot locate host plant)
      - Reduces whitefly landing and feeding
      - Practical for smaller plots; expensive at commercial scale
   
   iii. Border/Trap Crops:
      - Okra (bhindi) as trap crop around cotton border
      - Sunflower as barrier crop on windward side
      - Tomato as indicator plant — if virus on tomatoes nearby, cotton at risk
      - Remove and destroy infected trap crop plants before whitefly migrates
   
   iv. Neem-Based Repellent Sprays:
      - Neem oil 5 ml/liter + soap 2 ml/liter spray
      - Apply every 10–15 days in early season (pre-bud stage)
      - Repels whitefly from landing; disrupts nymph feeding
   
   v. Seed Treatment for Early Protection:
      - Imidacloprid 70% WS @ 5–7 g/kg seed
      - Protects seedlings for first 35–45 days from whitefly feeding
      - Crucial: Most CLCuV infection happens in first 30–45 days of crop
   
   vi. Avoid Alternate Host Management:
      - Remove Malvastrum (false mallow), Croton bonplandianum (Paatla),
        Wild okra, Parthenium, and other Malvaceae weeds from field edges
      - These harbor both whitefly populations AND CLCuV virus reservoir
      - Coordinate with neighboring farmers for community-level whitefly control
   
   vii. Insecticide Rotation (when whitefly exceeds 10 per leaf):
      - Avoid repeated use of same chemical class — resistance develops fast
      - Rotation example: 
        Week 1: Spiromesifen (Oberon) — kills eggs + nymphs
        Week 3: Pyriproxyfen (Admiral) — growth regulator
        Week 5: Flonicamid (Aria) — novel mode of action
        Week 7: Buprofezin (Applaud) — nymph-specific
      - NEVER use: Lambda-cyhalothrin (pyrethroids) as first choice — 
        kills natural enemies, causes whitefly resurgence

C. ROGUE INFECTED PLANTS EARLY:
   - Scout weekly from day 20 onwards
   - Remove and BURY (do not throw on field edge) any plant showing:
     * Mosaic + leaf curl + vein thickening together
   - Early removal reduces the virus reservoir in field
   - Even 5% infected plants can spread to 30–40% by season end if not removed

D. AVOID LATE SOWING:
   - Late-sown cotton is exposed to whitefly migration from earlier crops
   - Staggered sowing within a village = higher CLCuV spread risk
   - Pakistan: Community-level uniform sowing date avoids staggered 
     susceptibility windows (CCRI recommends village-level synchronized sowing)

3.2 PREVENTION OF ZINC DEFICIENCY VARIEGATION
-----------------------------------------------
  A. Pre-season Soil Zinc Testing:
     - Test for DTPA-extractable Zinc (target: >0.6 mg/kg)
     - In Pakistan: Most Punjab soils have <0.4 mg/kg Zn (deficient)
  
  B. Zinc Soil Application:
     - Apply Zinc Sulfate monohydrate (ZnSO4.H2O — 33% Zn) @ 5 kg/acre
     - Incorporate into soil before planting (mix in top 15 cm)
     - For severely deficient soils: Apply 10 kg ZnSO4/acre
     - Zinc residual effect: 2–3 years in soil — not needed every season
  
  C. Zinc Foliar Spray:
     - ZnSO4 0.5% (5 g/liter) spray at:
       * 3-leaf stage
       * Bud initiation stage
       * Flower opening stage
     - Add Lime (Chuna — CaO) 0.25% to ZnSO4 solution to prevent leaf burn
     - Chelated Zinc (EDTA-Zn) @ 0.1% — more efficient but more expensive

3.3 PREVENTION OF IRON DEFICIENCY VARIEGATION
-----------------------------------------------
  A. Improve Drainage (Most Important for Iron):
     - Waterlogged soils → anaerobic → iron uptake blocked
     - Install drainage furrows; avoid over-irrigation
  
  B. Reduce Irrigation Water Bicarbonate:
     - Pakistan canal water often has high bicarbonate → raises soil pH → 
       blocks iron uptake
     - Acidify irrigation water using Sulfuric acid (industrial grade) @ 
       0.5–1 ml per liter of irrigation water — raises water quality
     - Use drip + acid injection system (fertigation)
  
  C. Iron Foliar Application:
     - Chelated Iron (Fe-EDTA or Fe-EDDHA) @ 0.1–0.2% foliar spray
     - Apply 3–4 times at 10-day intervals when symptoms appear
     - FeSO4 (Ferrous sulfate) 0.5% + Citric acid 0.1% spray — cheaper option
  
  D. Acidifying Amendments:
     - Elemental Sulfur @ 5 kg/acre reduces soil pH gradually
     - Ammonium Sulfate as nitrogen source (acidifies root zone)

3.4 MANAGING MANGANESE DEFICIENCY (''-Specific)
----------------------------------------------------
  - Manganese Sulfate (MnSO4) @ 5 kg/acre soil application OR
  - 0.5% MnSO4 foliar spray, 2–3 times at 15-day intervals
  - Avoid high pH soils — if pH > 7.5, Mn availability drops sharply
  - Address pH with elemental sulfur application

3.5 VARIETY CERTIFICATION TO PREVENT GENETIC VARIEGATION
----------------------------------------------------------
  - Always purchase CERTIFIED SEED from government-approved sources
  - Pakistan: Punjab Seed Corporation, CCRI Multan, AARI certified seed lots
  - '': National Seeds Corporation, State Seeds Corporations
  - Avoid roadside vendors selling unlabeled or mixed variety seed
  - Certified seed ensures genetic purity → no chimeric/off-type plants
