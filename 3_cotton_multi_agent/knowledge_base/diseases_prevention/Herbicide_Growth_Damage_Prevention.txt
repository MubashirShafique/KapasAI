
1. OVERVIEW / INTRODUCTION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Herbicide Growth Damage, also called herbicide phytotoxicity or hormone weed killer 
injury, is one of the most commonly misdiagnosed problems in cotton fields across 
Pakistan and ''. It occurs when cotton plants are exposed to herbicide chemicals 
— either through direct application, spray drift from neighboring fields, soil 
residues, or contaminated irrigation water.

Cotton (Gossypium hirsutum) is EXTREMELY sensitive to auxin-type herbicides 
(2,4-D and dicamba being the most dangerous), and even minute quantities — as 
low as 1–5 parts per billion — can cause severe plant deformity, reduced yield, 
and in some cases complete crop loss.

In Pakistan, the problem is most severe in Punjab (Multan, Bahawalpur, Rahim Yar 
Khan divisions) and Sindh (Sanghar, Mirpur Khas). In '', it is widespread in 
Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan, and parts of Vidarbha (Maharashtra).

Economic Impact:
- Yield loss from herbicide drift alone: 10–60% depending on growth stage
- Cost of replanting: PKR 15,000–25,000/acre (Pakistan); INR 12,000–20,000/acre ('')
- Post-injury recovery is often incomplete — plants rarely return to full yield potential

2. TYPES OF HERBICIDE DAMAGE IN COTTON
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

2.1 Auxin-Type Herbicide Injury (Most Common & Most Severe)
------------------------------------------------------------
Herbicides: 2,4-D (butyl ester), Dicamba, MCPA, Triclopyr
Symptoms:
  - Severe leaf cupping, curling downward (epinasty)
  - Twisted, strap-like, elongated leaves ("strapping")
  - Swollen, cracked, or corky stems
  - Leaf petioles bending downward
  - Malformed bolls and boll shedding
  - Roots becoming club-shaped or stubby
  - Growing tip death in severe exposure

2.2 Acetolactate Synthase (ALS) Inhibitor Injury
-------------------------------------------------
Herbicides: Imazethapyr, Metsulfuron, Tribenuron-methyl, Chlorimuron
Symptoms:
  - Interveinal chlorosis (yellowing between leaf veins)
  - Stunted, compressed new growth
  - Purple/red discoloration in younger leaves
  - Slow plant death from growing point downward

2.3 PPO Inhibitor Injury (Contact Herbicides)
----------------------------------------------
Herbicides: Oxyfluorfen, Acifluorfen, Fomesafen
Symptoms:
  - Brown/tan necrotic spots on leaves (looks like burn)
  - Leaf edges dry and brittle
  - Usually confined to leaves present at time of exposure

2.4 Pre-emergent Soil Residue Damage
--------------------------------------
Herbicides: Pendimethalin, Trifluralin, Atrazine (from corn/wheat rotations)
Symptoms:
  - Poor germination
  - Stunted root system, roots turn brown
  - Seedlings die soon after emergence
  - Leaf yellowing starting from the base

3. CAUSES & RISK FACTORS IN PAKISTAN & ''
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

PRIMARY CAUSES:
  A. Spray Drift from Neighboring Fields
     - 2,4-D is widely used in wheat (gehun) grown next to cotton
     - Wind speeds above 8–10 km/h carry droplets 400–800 meters
     - Pakistan: Wheat-cotton crop rotation means neighboring sprays overlap
     - '': Sugarcane and wheat fields adjoining cotton = high drift risk

  B. Use of Contaminated Spray Equipment
     - Farmers use same sprayer for wheat herbicide AND cotton pesticides
     - Residues of 2,4-D can remain in pumps, hoses, nozzles even after washing
     - Even 0.1% contamination is enough to damage cotton

  C. Improper Herbicide Application Timing
     - Applying post-emergent herbicides during hot afternoon hours 
     - High temperatures volatilize ester forms of 2,4-D — vapors drift
     - Pakistan summers (45°C+): Butyl ester of 2,4-D becomes extremely volatile

  D. Soil Residues from Previous Crop
     - Atrazine (used in corn/maize) persists 12–18 months in heavy clay soils
     - Chlorimuron (soybean herbicide) has 6–12 month half-life in alkaline soils
     - Pakistan's calcareous (alkaline) soils in Punjab prolong herbicide persistence

  E. Misidentification of Herbicides
     - Illiterate or semi-literate farmers cannot read labels
     - Purchase counterfeit or mislabeled herbicides from local dealers
     - Apply wheat herbicide accidentally to cotton

  F. Irrigation Water Contamination
     - Canal water downstream from wheat fields can carry herbicide residues
     - Pakistan: Head/tail water distribution means variable exposure

4. PREVENTION STRATEGIES — DETAILED
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

4.1 DEDICATED SPRAY EQUIPMENT FOR COTTON
------------------------------------------
► NEVER use the same sprayer for herbicides and insecticides/fungicides
► Maintain SEPARATE sprayers labeled for:
   - "HERBICIDE USE ONLY" (painted RED)
   - "INSECTICIDE/FUNGICIDE ONLY" (painted BLUE or GREEN)
► If separate sprayer not available — Triple Rinse Protocol:
   Step 1: Empty sprayer completely
   Step 2: Fill with clean water, add 1% ammonia solution
   Step 3: Pump through all hoses and nozzles — let sit 15 minutes
   Step 4: Drain completely — repeat 3 times with fresh water
   Step 5: STILL not guaranteed safe for cotton — buy a separate one

4.2 PROPER SPRAY TIMING TO PREVENT DRIFT
------------------------------------------
► Apply herbicides in neighboring crops ONLY:
   - Early morning (before 8:00 AM) — low temperature, low wind
   - Evening (after 6:00 PM) — cooler temperatures, reduced volatility
► NEVER spray when wind speed exceeds 8 km/h toward cotton fields
► Check wind direction with a simple ribbon tied to a stick before spraying
► In Pakistan: Avoid spraying 2,4-D from March–April when cotton is being planted
   and young seedlings (first 45 days) are highly vulnerable
► In '': Kharif season cotton is most vulnerable June–August during wheat 
   stubble/maize herbicide applications nearby

4.3 USE AMINE FORM INSTEAD OF ESTER FORM
------------------------------------------
► 2,4-D Amine salt is FAR LESS volatile than 2,4-D Butyl Ester
► Amine form: low volatility, safe to use near cotton if applied carefully
► Ester form: HIGH volatility, NEVER use within 1 km of cotton fields
► Always read the label: "dimethylamine salt" = safer than "butyl ester" or "ethyl ester"
► Pakistan: Most cheap local 2,4-D sold is Butyl Ester — avoid near cotton

4.4 BUFFER ZONES / WINDBREAKS
--------------------------------
► Plant wind-breaking trees (shisham, eucalyptus, poplar/safeda) along 
   field boundaries facing the direction of prevailing winds
► Minimum buffer zone: 50 meters between wheat field being sprayed with 2,4-D 
   and cotton seedlings
► Pakistan: In Punjabi flat terrain, recommend 100-meter buffer given wind patterns
► Use guard rows of sorghum (jowar) or maize as physical drift barriers

4.5 CORRECT HERBICIDE SELECTION FOR COTTON
--------------------------------------------
SAFE herbicides for use WITHIN cotton fields (Pakistan/'' registered):
  - Pendimethalin (pre-emergent, apply before cotton emerges)
  - Trifluralin (incorporated pre-planting)
  - MSMA (mono-sodium methyl arsenate) — post-emergent, use carefully
  - Pyrithiobac-sodium (Staple) — selective in cotton, targets broadleaf weeds
  - Fluazifop-p-butyl — for grass weed control in cotton
  - Quizalofop-ethyl — graminicide, safe in cotton

ABSOLUTELY FORBIDDEN near cotton:
  - 2,4-D (any form, within 200m during growing season)
  - Dicamba (never use near cotton — extremely volatile)
  - Imazethapyr (unless in imidazolinone-tolerant "Clearfield" cotton varieties)
  - Atrazine (wheat/corn follow-crop residue risk)

4.6 SOIL TESTING BEFORE PLANTING
-----------------------------------
► Before planting cotton in fields previously used for maize/corn or wheat:
   - Test for herbicide residues using bioassay method:
     > Grow radish or canary grass seeds in soil sample in pots
     > If stunting occurs — soil has herbicide residues
     > Do NOT plant cotton in that field
► Pakistan Agriculture Research Council (PARC) and Ayub Agricultural Research 
   Institute (AARI) Faisalabad offer soil testing services
► '': ICAR-Central Institute for Cotton Research (CICR) Nagpur offers 
   residue analysis services

4.7 AWARENESS & LABEL READING CAMPAIGNS
-----------------------------------------
► Pakistan: Work with Department of Agriculture Extension officers to train 
   farmers on herbicide label reading
► Label symbols to watch for:
   - Green triangle = safe/low toxicity
   - Yellow diamond = moderate risk
   - Red circle with skull = high danger
► '': ATMA (Agricultural Technology Management Agency) conducts farmer 
   training — request herbicide safety workshop
► Village-level demarcation: erect signboards "Cotton Zone — No 2,4-D Spray"

4.8 CROP ROTATION MANAGEMENT
-------------------------------
► If field had atrazine-treated corn: Wait minimum 18 months before planting cotton
► If field had sulfonylurea herbicides (Metsulfuron, Chlorimuron): 
   Wait 12 months, especially in alkaline soils
► Grow a bioassay test crop (oats or sunflower) first to test soil safety
► Pakistan rotation: Wheat→Cotton is common — ensure wheat herbicide window 
   ends at least 8 weeks before cotton planting

4.9 WATER SOURCE MANAGEMENT
------------------------------
► Do not irrigate cotton from channels that have passed through recently 
   herbicide-treated wheat fields within 3–5 days of application
► Use settling ponds or allow 7-day holding before using canal water 
   downstream from herbicide application zones
► Test irrigation water pH — very high pH (>8.5) slows herbicide breakdown 
   in soil and water

5. EMERGENCY RESPONSE IF DAMAGE ALREADY OCCURRED
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

► Mild drift exposure (less than 30% plants affected, squiggly leaves):
   - Apply foliar spray of Gibberellic Acid (GA3) at 50–100 ppm
   - Apply Zinc Sulfate 0.5% foliar spray (zinc helps auxin metabolism)
   - Increase irrigation frequency to dilute soil residue
   - Plants often recover in 3–4 weeks during early vegetative stage

► Moderate damage (30–60% plants, severe cupping):
   - Apply Urea (1%) + Zinc Sulfate (0.5%) foliar spray
   - Add Thiourea (500 ppm) to reduce oxidative stress
   - Remove/prune severely deformed growing tips to encourage new growth
   - Continue monitoring for 3 weeks

► Severe damage (>60% plants, growing point dead):
   - Consider replanting if within first 30 days of crop
   - File complaint with agriculture extension for drift damage documentation
   - Pakistan: Contact District Agriculture Officer for compensation assessment

Month         Action
----------    ---------------------------------------------------------------
January       Test soil for herbicide residue from previous wheat crop
February      Select safe herbicides for weed control in wheat near future 
              cotton zones. Switch to 2,4-D Amine (not Ester)

April         Cotton planting begins — NO 2,4-D spray within 500m
May           High alert period — seedling stage, maximum sensitivity
June–August   If needed, use only cotton-approved herbicides (Pyrithiobac)
September     Pre-harvest defoliant use — follow label dosages strictly
October       Harvest — document any damage for insurance/compensation claims
November      Begin soil testing for next season
