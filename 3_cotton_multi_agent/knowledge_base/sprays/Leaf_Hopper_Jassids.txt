============================================================
DISEASE: LEAF HOPPER / JASSIDS (Tiddha) on Cotton (Kapas)
Region: Punjab & Sindh, Pakistan
============================================================

--- PEST IDENTIFICATION ---
Scientific Name : Amrasca biguttula biguttula (Ishida) — Cotton Jassid
                  Amrasca devastans (Distant) — also reported
Local Name       : Tiddha, Jassid, Chota Tiddha
Type             : Sucking pest (both nymphs and adults damage)
Affected Stage   : All stages; most damaging during vegetative to early boll stage

Symptoms:
- Wedge-shaped tiny green/yellow insects (2-3 mm); jump when disturbed
- Both nymphs and adults suck sap from underside of leaves
- Inject salivary toxins that DESTROY plant tissue (not just nutrient removal)
- HOPPER BURN: Leaf margins turn yellow, then red/brown, then curl downward (cup shape)
- Yellowing/browning starts at leaf edges and progresses inward (triangular burn)
- Severely affected leaves become leathery, crinkle, and dry
- Overall plant stunting, reduced flowering, boll shedding
- ETL (Economic Threshold Level): 1-2 jassids per leaf (young plant stage)
                                   2-4 per leaf at flowering/boll stage

--- CAUSES & FAVORABLE CONDITIONS ---
- Hot, dry weather (30-40°C) with LOW humidity (< 50%) = PEAK season
- April–August = Peak period in both Punjab and Sindh
- Dense planting increases jassid population
- Sandy/light soils with stressed plants more vulnerable
- Khairpur (Sindh) and Multan, Bahawalpur (Punjab) are endemic jassid zones
- Research from Khairpur, Sindh confirms Amrasca biguttula biguttula as dominant species

WEATHER-BASED SPRAY TIMING GUIDE (FOR RAG AGENT)
============================================================

CONDITION 1 — HOT & DRY (Temp: 30-42°C, Humidity: < 50%)
  → ★★★ PEAK JASSID SEASON — highest population and damage risk
  → Favorable temp range for jassids: 21-31°C (peaks around 35-38°C)
  → SPRAY: Confidor (Imidacloprid) or Actara (Thiamethoxam)
  → Spray time: EARLY MORNING (5:00-7:00 AM) or LATE EVENING (6:00-8:00 PM)
  → Do NOT spray in midday heat — chemical degrades faster; leaf absorption reduced
  → Irrigate field 24 hours before spraying — reduces plant stress and improves uptake
  → Add 0.2% spreader-sticker to improve coverage on waxy leaf surface
  → Check fields every 3-4 days; jassid populations can triple in 7 days in heat

CONDITION 2 — WARM & HUMID (Temp: 28-35°C, Humidity: 55-80%)
  → Jassid still active but natural enemies (parasitic wasps, spiders) also active
  → Check ETL before spraying: spray only if > 1-2 per leaf
  → SPRAY: Acetamiprid (Mospilan/Diamond) — less harmful to beneficial insects
  → Biological option: Neem spray if below ETL (preventive)
  → Morning spray (6-8 AM) preferred

CONDITION 3 — MONSOON RAIN SEASON (July–September, Sindh & Punjab)
  → Rain temporarily reduces jassid population
  → BUT: after rain, warm humid conditions cause RAPID population resurgence
  → Scout fields within 3-4 days after rain
  → If ETL crossed post-rain: Spray Polo (Diafenthiuron) — more rain-resistant
  → WP or SC formulations preferred over EC in humid conditions (better adhesion)
  → Spray 24-48 hours after rain (when leaves are dry)

CONDITION 4 — AFTER IRRIGATION
  → Irrigated cotton is more succulent — more attractive to jassids
  → Scout 3-5 days after each irrigation for jassid buildup
  → Preventive spray if previous cycle showed high population
  → Use systemic insecticide (Imidacloprid/Thiamethoxam) for 3-week protection

CONDITION 5 — WINDY WEATHER (> 15 km/h)
  → Do NOT spray — drift reduces efficacy; jassids hide beneath leaves
  → Wait for calm morning or evening hours
  → Jassids move to underside of leaves in wind — ensure spray reaches underside

CONDITION 6 — COLD WEATHER (Temp < 20°C at night)
  → Jassid activity slows significantly
  → Monitor only; spray not usually required below 20°C sustained temperatures
  → If temperatures rise again: immediate scouting required

SPRAY COVERAGE TIP FOR JASSIDS:
- Jassids live on UNDERSIDE of leaves — direct nozzle upward to reach under-leaf surface
- Use higher water volume (120-150 liters/acre) for thorough coverage
- Use fan nozzle rather than cone nozzle for better underside coverage

RESISTANCE MANAGEMENT ROTATION SCHEDULE (Pakistan)
============================================================
Spray Cycle 1: Imidacloprid (Confidor) — Neonicotinoid Group 4A
Spray Cycle 2: Diafenthiuron (Polo) — Different mode of action entirely
Spray Cycle 3: Acetamiprid (Mospilan/Diamond) — Neonicotinoid Group 4A
Spray Cycle 4: Thiamethoxam (Actara) — Neonicotinoid Group 4A
→ Rotate between Polo + Neonicotinoids; never use same chemistry 3× in a row

============================================================
INTEGRATED PEST MANAGEMENT (IPM) FOR JASSIDS
============================================================
- Use resistant varieties: BH-167, CIM-573, MNH-786 (Punjab); CRIS varieties (Sindh) show lower jassid susceptibility
- Reflective mulches: Reduce landing of jassids (silver/white plastic mulch)
- Yellow sticky traps: For monitoring (not mass trapping)
- Avoid excessive nitrogen — lush succulent plants attract more jassids
- Maintain natural enemies: Avoid calendar spraying; spray only at ETL
- Intercrop with coriander or marigold to attract beneficial insects
