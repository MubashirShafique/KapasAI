============================================================
DISEASE: LEAF REDDENING (Lal Patti / Lalai) on Cotton (Kapas)
Region: Punjab & Sindh, Pakistan
============================================================

--- DISEASE IDENTIFICATION ---
Type          : Physiological Disorder (Abiotic) — primarily nutritional/environmental
Local Name    : Lal Patti, Lalai, Patti Ka Lal Hona
Affected Stage: Flowering, Boll Development Stage (July–September most common)

Symptoms:
- Leaf margins turn red/purple first, then spreading to the rest of the blade
- Leaves develop reddish-purple discoloration due to anthocyanin pigment accumulation
- Chlorophyll (green pigment) decreases while red pigment (anthocyanin) increases
- Severely affected leaves dry and shed early (defoliation)
- Boll shedding and reduced boll weight follows severe reddening
- Plants may look stunted with fewer open bolls
- Reddening from margins inward = potassium/phosphorus deficiency
- Reddening between leaf veins = magnesium deficiency (veins stay green)
- Whole-leaf reddening + curling = could indicate viral cause; check for whitefly

Primary Causes in Pakistan Cotton:
1. POTASSIUM (K) Deficiency — most common cause; cotton is a high K-demand crop
2. NITROGEN (N) Deficiency — especially during boll load period
3. PHOSPHORUS (P) Deficiency — early season reddening
4. MAGNESIUM (Mg) Deficiency — interveinal reddening (veins remain green)
5. Water Stress (Drought) — reduces nutrient uptake, triggers reddening
6. Waterlogging — causes root death, blocks nutrient uptake
7. Sucking Pest Attack (Jassids) — toxin injection causes reddening
8. Environmental stress — cold nights, high heat, excessive sunlight
9. High Boll Load — simultaneous boll development depletes N, triggering reddening

Key Fact: Pakistan cotton soils are potassium deficient in many areas of Sindh and South Punjab,
making K deficiency-driven reddening extremely common.

============================================================
RECOMMENDED SPRAYS/TREATMENTS — PAKISTAN LOCAL MARKET
============================================================

PRIMARY TREATMENT — POTASSIUM DEFICIENCY REDDENING:
----------------------------------------------------
1. Potassium Sulphate (SOP) Foliar Spray — FIRST CHOICE
   Product    : Potassium Sulphate (0-0-50+17S) or (0-0-52)
   Local Names: SOP, Potash Sulphate
   Dose       : 200-250 g per 10 liters of water = 2-2.5% solution
   Per Acre   : 2-2.5 kg in 100 liters water
   Application: 3-4 foliar sprays at 7-10 day intervals
   Brands     : Engro SOP, Fauji SOP, Ittehad SOP — widely available in Punjab & Sindh
   Notes      : Foliar K corrects deficiency in as little as 20 hours; essential during boll stage

2. Potassium Nitrate (13-0-46) Foliar Spray — ALTERNATIVE
   Dose       : 250-300 g per 10 liters of water
   Brands     : Haifa Multi-K, Qrop K (various local distributors)
   Notes      : Supplies both K and N simultaneously; better for nitrogen + potassium combined deficiency
   Availability: Faisalabad, Lahore, Multan agri markets; Hyderabad, Sukkur in Sindh

NITROGEN SUPPLEMENT SPRAY:
---------------------------
3. Urea Foliar Spray
   Dose       : 10-15 g urea per liter of water (1-1.5% solution)
   Per Acre   : 1-1.5 kg urea in 100-120 liters water
   Application: 2-3 sprays at 10-day intervals
   Notes      : Provides fast nitrogen; prevents chlorophyll breakdown; widely available and cheap
   Important  : Do NOT use DAP as foliar (too high salt index; leaf burn risk); use urea only

4. DAP (Di-Ammonium Phosphate) Soil Application
   Dose       : 50 kg per hectare applied as soil dose
   Notes      : For phosphorus deficiency early season reddening; not foliar — soil application only
   Brands     : Engro DAP, FFC DAP — nationwide

MAGNESIUM SUPPLEMENT (For Interveinal Reddening):
--------------------------------------------------
5. Magnesium Sulphate (MgSO4 / Epsom Salt)
   Dose       : 20-25 g per 10 liters of water
   Per Acre   : 2-2.5 kg in 100-120 liters water
   Application: 2-3 sprays weekly
   Notes      : Apply soil @ 25 kg/hectare for chronic Mg deficiency soils
   Recovery   : 50-60% recovery in greenness reported within 2-3 sprays (MCV-5 variety trials)

COMBINED RECOVERY SPRAY (Best Practice):
-----------------------------------------
6. Urea (1%) + MgSO4 (0.5%) + SOP (1%) Combination
   Application: Mix all three; spray 2-3 times at 10-day intervals
   Notes      : This combination is widely recommended by Pakistan Agriculture Extension services
               for multi-nutrient deficiency reddening during boll stage

ANTI-STRESS PLANT GROWTH REGULATOR:
------------------------------------
7. Ascorbic Acid 500 ppm + PMA (Phenylmercuric Acetate) 10 ppm
   Notes      : Reduces oxidative stress; helps recovery when reddening caused by drought stress
   Availability: Specialty agri shops in Lahore, Karachi, Multan, Hyderabad

FOR PEST-INDUCED REDDENING (Jassid toxin):
-------------------------------------------
8. Imidacloprid (Confidor) + Urea Spray
   Dose       : Confidor 0.5 ml/L + Urea 1% (10 g/L) mixed together
   Notes      : Controls jassid (which causes toxin-induced reddening) + supplies nitrogen
               for leaf recovery simultaneously; spray at sign of jassid + reddening together

============================================================
WEATHER-BASED SPRAY TIMING GUIDE (FOR RAG AGENT)
============================================================

CONDITION 1 — HOT & DRY (Temp: 38-45°C, Humidity: < 40%, Soil Moisture LOW)
  → Most critical: drought stress + heat causing K and N depletion = MAXIMUM reddening risk
  → IMMEDIATE ACTION: Irrigate first, then spray 12-24 hours after irrigation
  → SPRAY: Potassium Sulphate (SOP) 2% + Urea 1% combination
  → Spray time: EARLY MORNING ONLY (5:00-6:30 AM) — avoid leaf burn in heat
  → Repeat spray every 7 days × 3-4 applications
  → DO NOT delay — each day without K causes 1-2% boll shedding increase

CONDITION 2 — BOLL STAGE WITH HEAVY BOLL LOAD (Aug–Sep in Punjab, Jul–Aug Sindh)
  → Peak reddening period — cotton pulls nitrogen + potassium from leaves to bolls
  → SPRAY: Potassium Nitrate (13-0-46) @ 2.5% solution — dual K and N
  → Spray every 7-10 days; minimum 3 applications
  → Apply additional urea soil top-dressing @ 25 kg/acre alongside foliar spray

CONDITION 3 — AFTER RAIN (Leaching risk in Sandy Sindh Soils)
  → Rain leaches potassium from sandy soils (common in Sindh)
  → Survey fields 3-4 days after heavy rain for reddening
  → SPRAY: SOP foliar spray within 5 days of rain
  → Add Humic Acid (500 ml/acre) to improve K retention in soil

CONDITION 4 — WATERLOGGED FIELD
  → Root death = blocked nutrient uptake = reddening
  → Drain field FIRST — no spray is effective on waterlogged plants
  → After drainage: apply Potassium Sulphate soil + foliar combined
  → Allow 3-5 days after drainage before assessing recovery

CONDITION 5 — COLD NIGHTS (Temp < 18°C at night)
  → Cold stress triggers anthocyanin (red pigment) accumulation
  → This is seasonal/natural reddening — less intervention needed
  → If early in season (May–June) and plants still young — apply Urea 1% spray
  → If late season (October+) — natural senescence; no intervention needed

CONDITION 6 — HUMID & WARM (Temp: 28-35°C, Humidity: 60-75%)
  → Best nutrient absorption conditions
  → Apply full treatment: SOP + Urea + MgSO4 combination spray
  → Morning (6-8 AM) for best stomatal uptake
  → Expect visible green recovery within 7-10 days of treatment

CONDITION 7 — OVERCAST / CLOUDY
  → Good spray conditions any time of day
  → Potassium and magnesium foliar sprays most effective (no UV breakdown)

============================================================
IDENTIFICATION KEY: REDDENING vs CURL VIRUS
============================================================
REDDENING (Abiotic):
✔ Affects older/lower leaves first
✔ Margins turn red then spread inward
✔ No leaf distortion or curling
✔ Spreads uniformly across field
✔ Improves with nutrient spray

CURL VIRUS (Biotic):
✗ Young upper leaves affected
✗ Leaves curl upward and thicken
✗ Veins engorge (pronounced vein swelling)
✗ Spreads from plant to plant (focus pattern)
✗ Does NOT improve with nutrient spray
→ If these signs present, refer to Curl Virus file (06_Curl_Virus.txt)

============================================================
SOIL MANAGEMENT
============================================================
- Apply Muriate of Potash (MOP) @ 50-75 kg/acre in soil at planting
- Gypsum (CaSO4) @ 500 kg/acre improves Ca and reduces K-Mg antagonism
- Apply FYM (Farm Yard Manure) to build soil organic matter
- Conduct annual soil test; target K > 120 ppm in soil
