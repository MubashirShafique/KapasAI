============================================================
DISEASE: APHIDS (Tela / Mahu) on Cotton (Kapas)
Region: Punjab & Sindh, Pakistan
============================================================

--- PEST IDENTIFICATION ---
Scientific Name : Aphis gossypii (Cotton Aphid)
Local Name (Urdu/Punjabi): Tela, Mahu, Cheppa
Affected Crop Stage : Seedling to Boll Development

Symptoms:
- Tiny soft-bodied insects (green/black/yellow) clustered under leaves and on growing tips
- Leaves curl downward and become sticky with honeydew
- Sooty mold (black fungus) develops on honeydew deposits
- Ants are attracted to honeydew (a reliable field indicator)
- Severe infestations stunt plant growth and reduce boll setting
- Economic Threshold Level (ETL): 20-25 aphids per leaf OR 10% infested plants

--- CAUSES & FAVORABLE CONDITIONS ---
- Hot, dry weather (35-42°C) with low humidity (Punjab summer peak)
- Excessive nitrogen fertilizer causing lush soft growth
- Early season (May–June) particularly dangerous in both Punjab and Sindh
- Absence of natural enemies (ladybird beetles, lacewings)

============================================================
WEATHER-BASED SPRAY TIMING GUIDE (FOR RAG AGENT)
============================================================

CONDITION 1 — HOT & DRY (Temp: 38-45°C, Humidity: <40%, Wind: <10 km/h)
  → Peak aphid season (May–July in Punjab; April–June in Sindh)
  → SPRAY: Imidacloprid (Confidor) or Thiamethoxam (Actara)
  → Spray time: Early morning (5:00-7:00 AM) or late evening (6:00-8:00 PM)
  → DO NOT spray during peak heat (10 AM–4 PM) — chemical breaks down faster
  → Water stress weakens plants; irrigate before spraying for better absorption

CONDITION 2 — WARM & HUMID (Temp: 28-35°C, Humidity: 60-80%)
  → Natural enemies (ladybirds) are active; check ETL before spraying
  → Spray only if ETL crossed: 20-25 aphids/leaf
  → SPRAY: Acetamiprid (Mospilan/Alpine) preferred — less harmful to beneficials
  → Spray timing: Morning (6:00-8:00 AM)

CONDITION 3 — AFTER RAIN OR IRRIGATION
  → Wait minimum 2-3 hours after rain before spraying
  → If rain expected within 2 hours — do NOT spray (chemical washes off)
  → Best window: 24-48 hours after rain (aphids resurge quickly in humid conditions)
  → Add sticker/spreader to spray solution in humid conditions

CONDITION 4 — WINDY CONDITIONS (Wind > 15 km/h)
  → Do NOT spray; drift will reduce efficacy and waste product
  → Wait for calm morning hours

CONDITION 5 — CLOUDY / OVERCAST (No direct sunlight)
  → Good window for spraying; UV degradation is lower
  → Systemic insecticides work well; spray any time during day

CONDITION 6 — COLD NIGHTS (Temp < 18°C at night)
  → Aphid activity slows; spray may not be needed
  → Monitor only; spray if daytime temp rises above 25°C

============================================================
RESISTANCE MANAGEMENT & ROTATION (CRITICAL FOR PAKISTAN)
============================================================
- Do NOT use the same chemical class more than 2 consecutive sprays
- Rotate: Group 1 (Neonicotinoid) → Group 2 (Organophosphate) → Group 1
- Resistance to Imidacloprid is developing in Punjab — rotate with Acetamiprid or Thiamethoxam
- Punjab farmers currently use 7-8 spray applications per season — reduce by using ETL-based decisions

============================================================
INTEGRATED PEST MANAGEMENT (IPM) TIPS
============================================================
- Preserve natural enemies: Avoid broad-spectrum sprays like Cypermethrin in early season
- Yellow sticky traps: Place 4-6 per acre for monitoring
- Avoid excess nitrogen fertilizer — reduces plant susceptibility
- Use resistant/tolerant varieties recommended by Punjab & Sindh Agriculture Departments
- Early sowing (April 1-15 in Punjab; March–April in Sindh) reduces peak aphid pressure
