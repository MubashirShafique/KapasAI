============================================================
DISEASE: ARMY WORM (Lashkar Keera) on Cotton (Kapas)
Region: Punjab & Sindh, Pakistan
============================================================

--- PEST IDENTIFICATION ---
Scientific Name : Spodoptera frugiperda (Fall Armyworm — FAW) & Spodoptera litura (Tobacco Armyworm)
Local Name       : Lashkar Keera, Sundi, Patta Khor
Affected Crops   : Cotton, Maize, Wheat, Vegetables
Peak Season      : July–October (Cotton bolling stage)

Symptoms:
- Young larvae (green/brown caterpillars) skeletonize leaves, eating between veins
- Older larvae (dark with stripes) eat entire leaf, leaving only mid-rib
- Mass movement of caterpillars in "army" across fields (hence the name)
- Irregular holes in leaves with ragged edges
- Frass (dark pellet droppings) visible on leaves and soil
- Damage appears suddenly and progresses rapidly
- ETL (Economic Threshold Level): 1-2 larvae per plant OR 5-10% leaf damage

--- CAUSES & FAVORABLE CONDITIONS ---
- Warm humid weather (25-35°C) with moderate rainfall
- Rainy season (July-September) triggers mass hatching in both Punjab and Sindh
- FAW (S. frugiperda) was newly reported in Pakistan in 2019 — now established in Punjab and Sindh
- Night-flying moths lay eggs in batches; eggs hatch in 2-3 days in hot weather
- Heavy infestations noted in Multan, Bahawalpur (Punjab) and Sukkur, Hyderabad (Sindh)

7. Lorsban 40 EC (Chlorpyrifos — Corteva/local equivalents)
   Active Ingredient : Chlorpyrifos 40%
   Dose              : 500-750 ml per acre (2.5-3.5 ml per liter of water)
   Notes             : Widely available; however, HIGH RESISTANCE reported in Punjab strains of FAW — use as last resort or in mixtures only; available in all markets

8. Karate / Talstar (Lambda-Cyhalothrin or Bifenthrin — Syngenta/FMC local)
   Active Ingredient : Lambda-Cyhalothrin 5% EC or Bifenthrin 10%
   Dose              : 300 ml per acre
   Notes             : Fast knockdown; pyrethroid resistance developing; mix with Emamectin for better control

IMPORTANT RESISTANCE WARNING (2025):
- Research from University of Punjab (2025) confirmed FAW resistance to:
  Cypermethrin (540x resistance ratio), Chlorpyrifos (222x), Emamectin Benzoate (91x)
- Rotate chemical classes every spray; do NOT rely on single chemistry
- Diamides (Coragen) + Emamectin rotation is currently the recommended strategy

============================================================
WEATHER-BASED SPRAY TIMING GUIDE (FOR RAG AGENT)
============================================================

CONDITION 1 — WARM & HUMID / AFTER RAIN (Temp: 25-35°C, Humidity: 60-80%)
  → PEAK ARMYWORM SEASON — check fields daily
  → Larvae are active at night; spray in EVENING (6-8 PM) for best control
  → SPRAY: Emamectin Benzoate (Proclaim) or Coragen
  → Add a good spreader-sticker in humid conditions
  → If larvae in stage 1-2 (small, green, up to 1 cm) — Spinosad is sufficient
  → If larvae in stage 3-4 (large, dark, > 2 cm) — Use Emamectin or Coragen; higher dose

CONDITION 2 — DRY & HOT (Temp: 38-45°C, Humidity: < 40%)
  → Armyworm activity reduced in extreme heat
  → Spray early morning (5-7 AM) only
  → Soil moisture stress weakens plants — irrigate, then spray after 12 hours
  → Chemical residual reduced in heat; may need re-spray in 7-10 days

CONDITION 3 — AFTER HEAVY RAIN (>25 mm)
  → Larvae wash to ground — re-infestation from soil
  → Wait 24 hours after rain, then spray thoroughly (soil + foliage)
  → Use Chlorpyrifos soil spray + foliar Emamectin combination
  → Scout fields within 48 hours of rain for new egg masses

CONDITION 4 — MONSOON CONTINUOUS RAIN
  → Spray is difficult; prioritize mechanical control (egg collection)
  → Use waterproof formulations (SC, WG, WP better than EC in rain)
  → Spray in dry weather windows (morning gaps between showers)

CONDITION 5 — CLOUDY BUT DRY
  → Best spray window for pyrethroids + emamectin combination
  → Spray afternoon (3-5 PM) when larvae start moving up the plant

CONDITION 6 — DUST STORM / HIGH WIND (> 20 km/h)
  → Do NOT spray; wait for calm conditions
  → Larvae shelter during storms; spray after storm passes (next morning)

============================================================
RESISTANCE MANAGEMENT ROTATION SCHEDULE
============================================================
Spray 1 (First Sighting): Spinosad (Tracer) — morning spray
Spray 2 (7 days later if needed): Emamectin Benzoate (Proclaim)
Spray 3 (if outbreak severe): Coragen (Chlorantraniliprole)
Do NOT: Use Chlorpyrifos or Cypermethrin alone — resistance too high in Punjab

============================================================
INTEGRATED PEST MANAGEMENT (IPM)
============================================================
- Pheromone traps: Place 5-6 per acre for FAW (Spodoptera frugiperda) monitoring
- Egg parasitoids: Trichogramma wasps — release 50,000 per acre (available from Punjab Agriculture Dept)
- Biological spray: Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) sprays (DiPel WP) — effective on young larvae
- Hand-picking: Effective in small fields; early morning when larvae are on upper leaves
- Light traps: Attract adult moths at night; reduce egg-laying population
